## Slide 2 — The line

“Let me go shop this for you.”

## Slide 3 — Why silence costs more than the software

Homeowners premiums increased **12%** in 2025. Auto increased **23%** in 2024.

Among agents we interviewed, about **15%** of customers churn each year.

Saving **one customer a month** from churning rescues about **$566** in commissions.

If I save you one customer a month, this software pays for itself.

**Notes:** Loss aversion, not upside. Do not walk the 5× ROI sheet. Do not lead with referrals. If they ask what one point of retention is worth at sale, that is the *second* beat — save it for slide 13. The $566 is Austin’s midsize-agency sheet at ~1,000 households; at 1,500 it is higher. Keep the spoken line simple.

---

## Slide 4 — How renewal works today

Review upcoming renewals → call and hope they pick up → hope you ask the right questions → shop → call back with a recommendation.

Agents can spend up to **2.5 hours** just to shop one client.

| Review the AMS | Call the client | Shop the portals | Recommend |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10–20 min / day | 10–15 min / customer | 1–2 hours / customer | 10–15 min / customer |
| Brian’s auto is up 17%. Home is up 24%. | If they answer. | 80–130 data points per carrier. | Walk the options. Bind it yourself. |

**Notes:** The grind is the shopping. Everything else they already know how to do. You are buying back the 1–2 hours.

---

## Slide 5 — It is a capacity problem

You have about **1,500** clients. **30** renew every week.

At the same time: the phone is ringing, service requests are coming in, claims need attention, and new business — usually referrals — needs a same-day response.

This is not a motivation problem. It is a capacity problem.

The work that builds trust is the work that gets skipped.

**Notes:** “So be honest — how many of those 30 hear from you before the bill?” Let them answer. Then: Upline is how those 30 get touched without adding headcount.

---

## Slide 6 — Meet Upline

Upline handles the prep, outreach, shopping, and follow-through.

You show up, build trust, and close.

Not a CRM. Not another tool you have to manage. A teammate that drives the work.

**Notes:** One sentence, then go product by product. Do not dump a feature list.

---

## Slide 7 — Four things, in order

1. **Prioritized outreach** — who to call this week, in your voice.
2. **Household questionnaire** — only the gaps, plus the next line of coverage.
3. **Shopping** — you do it, or we do it.
4. **Recommendation** — the winner, the why, the email. You close.

**Notes:** Read them as products, not features. Each of the next four slides has to stand alone. Outreach is still a win if nobody fills out the questionnaire.

---

## Slide 8 — Prioritized outreach

Upline pulls your book and your renewals from the AMS. It surfaces who needs attention this week — rate holding, dropping, or jumping.

Then it drafts a personalized email in your voice.

Even if they never fill anything out, they heard from you before the bill arrived.

> Your homeowners with Progressive renews this month. The premium is up 12%. Progressive homeowners in Pennsylvania are up about 19% this year — so you are below average, but it is still real money. If you want, I can shop it with our other carriers.

**Notes:** This is the first sale. Personalized, prioritized, feels like the agent. Nail this even if shopping never happens. Human in the loop: they review and send. That is also the compliance story.

---

## Slide 9 — Household questionnaire

We look at everything you know about the household, and everything you don’t. Then we ask only for the gaps.

- Current drivers and vehicles
- Roof, dogs, pool, other underwriting changes
- Updated contact info

And like any good salesperson, we ask for the next piece of coverage: home, umbrella, life.

**Notes:** The questionnaire is how you get from “I emailed them” to “I can actually shop them.” Cross-sell is not a separate campaign. It lives on this form, at the moment they are already paying attention.

---

## Slide 10 — Shopping

You can shop it. Your VAs can shop it. Or we can.

When we shop:

- **$18** per policy
- Up to **three or four** carriers — the right ones for that household, not just your usual top three
- **24-hour turnaround, or you don’t pay**

Most agencies spend about two hours in the portals. Fully loaded, that is closer to **$32** a shop. We are about half that.

Carrier-agnostic. Your panel. You still bind.

**Notes:** Keep shopping as its own line item. Do not bundle it into the monthly. The 24-hour guarantee is untested language — say it, watch the face. If they flinch on $18, anchor first: “What does your CSR make fully loaded, and how long does a shop take?” Do not negotiate ourselves down to $12 on the first ask.

---

## Slide 11 — Recommendation

We bring you the winner: who came out ahead, what it costs, and why.

Then we draft the email in your voice and put it on a comparison page the client can actually read.

You review. You send. You walk them through it. You rewrite and bind.

The relationship stays yours. The grind does not.

**Notes:** Collaborator, not replacement. Bind stays with the agency. If they want to stay with the incumbent, that is a win too — they heard from you.

---

## Slide 12 — Life, if you want it

The offer to shop is a conversation starter. Once the door is open:

- “While we are reviewing coverage, can I get you a quick quote on life?”
- “You’re only carrying auto — want to look at bundling home?”
- “Is there anyone in your circle who could use this kind of attention?”

Most agencies are not selling life today. If you want us to, we handle the details. You stay the relationship. You are eligible for **60%** of that commission.

You do not have to. The software does not need life to pay for itself.

**Notes:** Optional. Do not discount the monthly to $499 in this version. If they already have a life plan, we just do not run the ask.

---

## Slide 13 — What it adds up to

Because clients see you looking out for them:

- **Retention goes up.** They do not drift when someone is watching.
- **Lines per household go up.** Cross-sell happens at the right moment, not never.
- **Referrals get asked.** Long-tenured clients actually hear the question.

You are not just adding this year’s revenue. You are building a book that sells at a higher multiple.

**Notes:** Second beat, after “one customer pays for this.” Two points of retention compounds — you keep people, they renew, the pond gets deeper. Use book-value language only after the simple line has landed. Do not put a referral dollar figure on the slide. If they ask: one referred household, five years, is on the order of $3,000 in commission — and it is the least predictable number we have.
