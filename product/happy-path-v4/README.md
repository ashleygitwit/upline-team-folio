# Happy path v4 (Amanda, Sept 30)

A clickable prototype of the agent's week: v3 (`product/happy-path-v3`, **Amanda_v3_sept_28** on Through Line) with the homepage rebuilt as a kanban board, for Stockton Hill and Members First on Friday, October 2. Through Line shows it as **Amanda_v4_sept_30** under Prototype.

This is a prototype, not the product. Everything is canned data in `src/data.ts`, `src/household/data.ts` and `src/pipeline.ts`, and all of it is invented: Harbor Point Insurance, Jenna Ruiz and every household were renamed on 2026-09-29, before the prototype went public on Through Line, so nothing here is a real agency, agent or policyholder. Every page carries `noindex`, and Through Line's `robots.txt` keeps crawlers out.

## What changed from v3

On 2026-09-30 the team settled on a kanban for the MVP homepage, and Stockton Hill clarified how many renewals would be in the pipeline at once. v4 shows that volume so we can get a feel for how much an agent takes in at one glance (Amanda's sketch of the columns and counts drove it).

- **The board replaces Action Needed and Scheduled Emails.** Under the header, six columns fill the rest of the screen, and each scrolls inside itself: **Scheduled**, **Awaiting Response**, **Shopping**, **Recommendation Ready**, **Recommendation Sent** and **Completed** (`src/board.ts`, `src/screens/upline/Board.tsx`). The sketch's "Proposal" became "Recommendation", on the columns only; the drawer and the modals keep their wording. Each column is the hub's quiet panel (gray 100, no border), with its name at the hub's column-heading size (20px) and its count beside it. The column headers share one row, so when "Awaiting Response" or "Recommendation Ready" wraps to two lines, every column's first line still starts at the same height. Any column folds to a strip from the control in its header, and stays folded for the rest of the walk. All six fit from about 1420 wide; narrower (a 1280 laptop, say), the board scrolls sideways inside itself with Completed past the edge, and folding any one column brings it back.
- **The first three columns are mini.** Nothing in them needs the agent: the emails go on their own, answers come in on their own, and Upline shops on its own. Each household is one line in a white list that runs the panel's width: the name and the change in percent, at 14px, the hub's floor for body text, and nothing else, so a column of 55 reads as a list of names (the carrier's mark and the three-dot menu came off on 2026-09-30). A line with an accent adds its renewal chip under the name, in the accent's color. A long name wraps instead of being cut short, and a couple's first names stay together ("Leah & Tom / Pruitt"). The whole line opens the household's drawer, where the renewal email is a click away in the banner or Recent activity.
- **The last three are short cards.** Each card has the name with the change in percent beside it, then its chips, led by the renewal chip (below), then the lines and carrier, last year's price to this year's in the mono face, one sentence and the action if there is one. Everything is 14px, as the kit's own rows are, with the name set apart by the display face and weight; the hub's rule is that sizes near each other differ by 1.25 or are the same. The shop's preview came off the cards (it's in the results), so three or four cards show per column instead of one and a half. The sentences are short: "Auto-Owners came in $1,050 less for the same coverage." Recommendation Sent is where renewals stall while the clock runs down, so each card says what was recommended and when it went. Once the household says yes, its card carries the Ready to close chip the Monday email uses, what they approved, and View profile and close. Closing it out moves it to Completed, with Jenna's note and Undo. The whole card opens the household's drawer; there's no three-dot menu (it came off on 2026-09-30), so Snooze is the clock at the end of the drawer's blue banner. A snoozed card sinks to the foot of its column, loses its accent, and says so, with Undo (it's no longer faded, since faded text fell under contrast).
- **An accent down the left edge says what needs a look.** It's the hub's card-accent, a 3px line that replaces the card's left hairline (on a mini line, the list's): **red** when the renewal is five days out or less and it isn't done, **yellow** when it's ten or less, and **blue** when the household has asked for something on top of the renewal, a life quote or a change on file. Blue means that and nothing else (since 2026-10-01; it used to mark a recommendation to send or an approval to bind, which the column and the Ready to close chip already say). Red beats yellow beats blue; Completed and snoozed carry none (`accentFor` in `src/board.ts`). It answers Megan's ask at the Stockton Hill review for red, yellow and green: red is the hub's error, yellow (a ramp the hub leaves unassigned) is warning here, and green, already the Agent's actor color, isn't used. Yellow was the hub's orange until 2026-10-01. Every card carries a **renewal chip**, and so does every mini line with a red or yellow accent, in the accent's color, so the bar says what it means: red and yellow count down ("Renews in 4 days"), and otherwise the chip is gray and gives the date ("Renews Nov 15") (`renewalChip` in `src/board.ts`). A blue bar is explained by the household's own blue chip instead. The chips are filled, red 500 and yellow 500, the same as their bars, both with slate 800 type (4.69:1 and 12.26:1). White on red 500 is 3.77:1 and the foreground on it 3.87:1, under the 4.5:1 the chip's 12px type needs, so the type is slate 800 on both. The red was red 700 with white until 2026-10-01. Separately, a change over 10% (the shop-framing threshold from the M1 learnings) is set in red 700, the red that carries text on white (red 500 as text on white would be 3.77:1).
- **Search and Needs me.** The toolbar over the board has **Needs me**, which keeps what needs Jenna: recommendations to send, approvals to bind, and anything with an accent (23 on Monday) (`needsYou` in `src/board.ts`), and a name search. Both narrow every column at once, and a column's count then says how many of its whole it's showing ("3 of 48"). They're the page's own, so they start clear at every stop.
- **The volume is Stockton Hill's.** A book of about 2,500 policies over 52 weeks is about 48 renewal emails a week, with about 55 waiting on an answer and about 41 between shopping and done, roughly 10 a column. The named twelve keep their files and walk. About 180 invented households fill the rest (`src/pipeline.ts`, from a fixed seed, so they land in the same places on every load). One in ten comes in flat, most go up in single digits and about a third by 10% or more. They have no drawer, so their lines and cards don't open, and a tooltip says "This household isn't built out for the prototype." when pointed at. They move through the week as the Pruitts do. Tuesday's emails are waiting from Wednesday. Two of them answered Tuesday night, as Leah did, and reach Recommendation Ready with the Pruitts on Thursday. The waiting pool's soonest renewals answer and get shopped. Recommendation Ready drains from 10 on Monday to empty on Wednesday.
- **The header replaced the blue band.** "Good morning, Jenna" and the day's line sit left-aligned on the page's edge, with the room above them the v3 Policyholder List had (the title 100px under the bar), and the toolbar and board follow at that page's gaps. The line counts off the board ("You have 10 recommendations ready to send and 4 policies to bind. Tomorrow at 9 AM, 48 renewal emails go out."; on Wednesday, "Nothing to send or bind today."). Upline's bar runs the page's full width too, so the mark, the greeting and the board's first column share an edge.
- **The drawer says the board's column.** The stage over a household's name in its drawer, and the eyebrow on its phase modals, are the board's column names (`src/household/columns.ts`, `src/household/activity.ts`): Scheduled, Awaiting Response, Shopping, Recommendation Ready, Recommendation Sent (an approval waiting to be bound too, as its card sits there) and Completed. They were Ashley's v2 names (Ready to reach out, Reached out, Ready to send rec, Rec sent, Closing, Closed). Send now on Monday sends the email then, so that household moves to Awaiting Response on the board, as its drawer says; it used to stay in Scheduled with a check.
- **The Policyholder List is gone.** The board is the whole book now, so the page, the band's link and My Policyholder List in Jenna's menu came out. So did the three households who left, who were only on that page.
- **The Monday email is the board at 8:00 AM.** Its Action Needed lists what Needs me keeps, in three groups: Ready to close (4), Recommendations ready to send (10) and Renewing soon (8, inside ten days and still open), each line with the board's accent and its renewal chip in the same color, under one sentence that counts them. Its Scheduled Emails lists the same 48 as the board's Scheduled column, biggest increase first by percent (Tobi Adeyemi's 22%, then the Pruitts' 18%), with the change in percent as the board shows it. It reads the same whatever the presenter does later.
- **The nudge list came off the homepage.** From Wednesday, Scheduled holds next week's emails, and the nudges and follow-ups open from each household's drawer, as they already did.

## Carried over from v3

## Before coffee

Looking over Monday's renewals is a task agents don't do today, and we're asking them to do it before 9:00 AM on a Tuesday. So the Monday email and the homepage have to be enjoyable before the first coffee: quick, painless, and doable while checking the inbox over breakfast. A greeting, one sentence and the way to the whole book; nothing that needs thinking to find.

## What changed from v2, and why

The review's direction was fewer things on screen, said in words, one action at a time (RAMP as the north star).

- **A slate card comes before each new day, each change of person and Jenna's first sign-in.** After BrightFuture's demo (brightfuture-demo.vercel.app/2), seven cards set up what comes next, so the screen after one reads as a different day, a different place or someone else's: before the Monday email, Jenna signing in to Upline from it, Leah's inbox on Tuesday, Jenna's Wednesday and Thursday, Leah's recommendation on Thursday evening, and Jenna's Friday. Each is the stage's slate 700 with a story title in the eyebrow, the day in a chip and a sentence in the display face, fading up as it arrives. There's no rule beside the title, as BrightFuture has, because blue 600 barely shows on slate and the stage's own captions go without one. Leah's inbox-to-questionnaire runs on without a card, since she's going straight from one screen to the next. The cards' words are fixed in `src/walk.ts` and tell the usual path; if the presenter skipped the Pruitts, the screen after Tuesday's card is the one that says so. In the presenter bar they're stops like any other, and Jump to heads each group of screens with its card: pick the card to set the stage, or a screen to skip past it. The Monday email's buttons land on the sign-in card and the questionnaire's closing link on Wednesday's, while Monday's link to the questionnaire still goes straight to it.
- **The week starts with an email, not a login.** Upline writes to the agent on Monday with everything that needs them that week. It opens on the design hub homepage's band (the grained mountain on blue 600, drawn from the hub's own files) with the white logo, "Good morning, Jenna" and "Here's what needs your attention this week." Under it, the week's to-dos: **Action Needed**, one card holding what the board's Needs me keeps (above), in three groups, Ready to close, Recommendations ready to send and Renewing soon, each soonest renewal first, with one View in Upline button across the foot, in primary blue. In v3 it was the earlier weeks' four: Rhea Iyer and Diane Mercer, approved and waiting to be bound, and Sofia Marin and Walter Kemp, shopped and ready for review. Anything renewing inside a week says how long is left ("Renews in 4 days") rather than the date. **Scheduled Emails** comes last, as one quiet card with no button, because the week's 48 renewal emails need nothing from Jenna: outreach still sends Tuesday at 9:00 AM if she does nothing, as the strategy sprint decided. They were two sections, Closing and Shopped, until the 2026-09-29 review found the split more than it was worth.
- **The homepage opens on the same header every day, and it only talks.** v3 had the design hub homepage's blue band here, as the Monday email opens; v4 sets the greeting and the line left-aligned on the page instead (above). It never holds a card or a button, bar the toolbar under it: a greeting and one line on what's going on (v3 also had a link to the Policyholder List). The greeting is "Good morning, Jenna", and "Happy Friday, Jenna" on Friday. The line follows the day and what the presenter has done: Monday counts off the board (above); Wednesday says "Nothing to send or bind today. This week's renewal emails went out Tuesday, and the Pruitts are being shopped."; Thursday, the walk's seventh stop, says "Shopping is done for Leah and Tom Pruitt and 2 others." and, once the recommendation has gone, "Your recommendation went to Leah and Tom. We'll let you know when they answer."; Friday says "Leah and Tom said yes to Auto-Owners. Bind it in the Auto-Owners portal before November 15, then close it out from their card below." and, once closed, "Done. The Pruitts are set." with what happens next. If the Pruitts were skipped, Thursday and Friday say "Nothing to send or bind today."
- **Under the header, the board** (above). In v3 this was two sections, Action Needed and Scheduled Emails, drawn as the Monday email draws them. On Monday the board holds, as Ashley's v2 board did, Rhea Iyer and Diane Mercer, approved and waiting to be bound, in Recommendation Sent; Sofia Marin and Walter Kemp, whose recommendations are ready to send, in Recommendation Ready; and Neha Rao and Marcus Yates in Shopping. On Thursday the Pruitts' card leads Recommendation Ready (Home + Auto · Erie · Renews Nov 15, "Auto-Owners came in $1,050 less for the same coverage."), and its View the full report opens their shop results in a modal while the rest of the card opens their drawer; on Friday their approval is in Recommendation Sent. Every card and line opens its household's drawer; v3's three-dot menus (View Profile, View Email, Snooze, Report an Error) came off on 2026-09-30.
- **Snooze puts a task off, and a quiet line keeps it in sight.** Austin's ask at the 2026-09-29 review, as the first step toward task management: the clock at the end of the drawer's blue banner offers Until tomorrow, Until next week and Until 5 days before renewal (the last isn't offered once the renewal is inside five days). A snoozed card sinks to the foot of its column and loses its accent, with Undo (in v3 it left the list for a Snoozed line); the drawer's banner turns gray and says "Snoozed until next week." with Undo; Recent activity logs it; and the household wears a Snoozed chip. Tomorrow's snooze is over at the next stop, next week's outlasts the walk, and the renewal's lets go five days before it (`src/tasks.ts`). - **Chips say what's pinned to a household, wherever it appears.** On the board's cards and lines and in the drawer's header (`src/components/Chips.tsx`). **Life quote requested** and **Info updated** are the blue ones, since blue means the household asked for something, and either gives the card a blue accent when nothing more pressing does (`changeRequested` in `src/tasks.ts`). - **Each phase's work opens in a modal, with its buttons in the footer.** They're all drawn the same way (`src/household/phases.tsx`): the drawer's header and ground, the work scrolling under it, and the footer under the scroll. ## Run it


## Publish it to Through Line

```bash
npm run export
```

Builds one self-contained HTML file (fonts included, no network needed) into `internal-comms/public/prototype-v4.html`.

## Capture it for the feedback board

With the dev server running:

```bash
npm run capture
```

Writes every screen and state (the sixteen stops, and everything that opens from each: Jenna's menu, the drawer's tabs and banners, the phase modals, a folded column, Needs me and a search) into `captures/` as PNGs, 1440 wide at 2×, with the presenter bar cropped off and every scrolling region opened out to its full height except the board, which is one screen by design, plus a `manifest.json` saying what each one is and how it was reached. It drives the Chrome already on the Mac through Playwright, so there's no browser download. `captures/` is ignored by git. v3's PNGs live on the FigJam board (Upline Design Feedback V3, the "Full Prototype Walk Through" page); v4 doesn't have a board of its own yet. `npm run capture 12` recaptures only the slugs starting with `12`.

`node scripts/figjam-layout.mjs <folder>` turns the manifest into the Figma Plugin API code that lays the board out, one file per stop, for running through the Figma MCP's `use_figma`; each file returns its image frames' ids, which `upload_assets` fills with the PNGs. The list of states is `captures` in `scripts/capture.mjs`; add a state there when the prototype grows one.
