## 1. What Upline is

**Decided — we are the servicing half of the agency, not a tool.** Divide an agency into producing
and servicing. Producing is the human on the golf course building relationships. Servicing is
everything that happens after, and that is us. The working analogy is COO or back-office concierge:
"Don't worry about it, we've got this."

**Decided — the promise of the independent agent is service, and the trap is that nobody can
deliver it.** An agency wins you with high-touch trust during the sale, then cannot sustain it,
because sustaining it takes expensive humans and software built for humans. That gap is the company.

**Decided — let the human be human, let the AI be the concierge.** This is the phrase we use. It
governs product decisions: anything that makes the agent feel like a salesperson or a data-entry
clerk is wrong.

**Decided — we do not come out as an AMS.** The category move is that there is no need for an AMS,
not that we are a better one. Accepting the AMS frame puts us in a feature-parity trap. Login should
not look like a system of record.

**Decided — we never position as "Upline Recommends™."** We show our work. The agent makes the
recommendation and the agent's name is on it.

**Decided — no AI persona fronting the relationship.** The idea of an AI concierge that emails the
policyholder directly and gives the agent plausible deniability ("that thing does that") was raised
Thursday and rejected. It undermines the human-relationship narrative and makes us look like another
direct-to-consumer play. Our job is to coach the agent to make a great offer, not to give them
someone to blame.

## 2. Who we sell to

**Decided — independent P&C agency owners who cannot run proactive renewal work account by account.**
They have accepted failure in personal lines: they know they are losing business, they know chasing
it is a grind, and they have stopped trying to fix it.

**Decided — commercial ambition is a buying signal, not a disqualifier.** A large share of owners
want to move into commercial and know their personal-lines process has to be shored up first so they
can go do it. That tension is our opening.

**Decided — three groups are explicitly not the first ICP:**

- **Very small shops (two-person).** Interest is real but implementation bandwidth is not. Nobody to
  onboard, train, or retain us, and if they do name someone, that person may leave.
- **Large or commercial-heavy books.**
- **Buyers who want AMS replacement on day one.**

**Noted — the champion and the skeptic are often the same person.** They want the leverage and they
do not want bad tech between them and their customer. Plan the pitch for one person holding both.

## 4. Pricing and contracting

This is the biggest change of the week. **The flat SaaS fee we have been pitching is dead.**

**Decided — we price as a percentage of personal-lines premium, not a flat monthly fee.** The pitch
is alignment: "I want to be completely incentivized to grow your revenue. If you shop your book and
lose revenue, I lose money." A percentage is a tier without hard edges — it scales perfectly instead
of jumping at a threshold.

**Decided — personal lines premium only.** Commercial is excluded from the basis.

**Decided — we do not do SaaS, we do partnerships.** This is the language. It also solves a real
messaging problem: we tell people we are not another SaaS tool and then hand them SaaS pricing.

**Decided — 24-month agreement, no implementation fee, billing starts at the end of month one.**

**Decided — the uplift guarantee replaces a 90-day out.** Rather than "cancel if you don't see
value," we guarantee we increase their retention rate. It gives them room to sign a 24-month deal
having seen us do nothing yet. The threshold number is still to be set.

**Decided — unlimited seats, priced at the agency level.** Outreach goes out under producers and
CSRs. Charging per seat would kill adoption among exactly the people who feed the system its data.

**Decided — shopping is all-in or all-out per agency.** We either shop for you or we do not. This
eliminates cherry-picking, and it is easier to explain and to sell. Note this is offering *the
opportunity* to shop to everyone, not shopping everyone.

**Decided — premium is self-reported at the sales call and confirmed after AMS access.** Owners know
their premium and their PIF off the top of their head. Three ranges on a slide ("sounds like you're
in the middle tier, we'll confirm once we have access") gets us to a one-meeting close without
turning tier assignment into a due-diligence project for our team.

**Direction set — two packages to test:**

| | Basis | Shopping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Option A** | 0.3% of personal-lines premium | + $18 per shop |
| **Option B** | ~0.5% of personal-lines premium | included |

**The percentage is not settled, and Option B is probably underpriced.** On a Members First-sized
book, 0.5% is about $25K, of which roughly $17K goes to shopping cost, leaving about $8K. Austin's
read: "It doesn't feel right… it's actually like 0.6 or 0.7." A fully loaded VA cost model is owed
before we quote this to anyone. Nobody is anchored to any of these numbers, and the instruction was
to get aggressive rather than undersell.

**Context for the conversation:** average first-line P&C commission is about 13.6% of premium. So
0.5% of premium is roughly 3.6% of the agency's revenue, and 0.3% is roughly 2.2%. That is the frame
an owner will actually evaluate.

**Decided — the shopping fee is a cost we pass through, not a profit center.** "We don't make any
money on the shop." It also gives us a standing reason to build AI shopping fast: when we land it,
the price does not drop to zero, it drops to 0.4% and we share the gain.

**Decided — we guarantee a 24-hour shop or it is free.** Flagged with care: this is already in the
sales deck and the VA system is not yet designed to deliver it.

**Held in reserve — a $5,000 implementation fee** as an objection-handler if someone pushes hard for
a 90-day trial, given what we invest in VA training and system access.

**Superseded — $699/mo plus $18 per shop.** Still in the Tuesday deck. Needs to come out. The $18
survives as our internal cost basis per shop, not as a line item we quote.

**Decided — November 6 is the ship date.** Everything below is scoped to it.

**Decided — we are in pared-down mode, and it should hurt.** "If it doesn't hurt, you're not going
far enough." The failure mode we are actively watching for is a black-hole feature that eats 60% of
the build.

**Above the line — what has to exist for customer one:**

- The renewal queue
- Outreach review and send
- The household questionnaire
- VA shopping of three carriers
- Shop results for the agent
- The proposal the policyholder sees
- A way to see who still owes a human action, so "needs binding" cannot silently disappear

**Below the line — decided and cut:**

- **The business intelligence dashboard as a screen.** Cut Thursday. The stats ship as an email at
  launch, and the screen is first thing back after. Worth knowing this one carried a magic-moment
  star and still got cut.
- Financial detail (premium, revenue, commission) in owner reporting — we will not have access to
  most of it.
- Native mobile app.
- "Ask Upline anything" chat overlay. Not worth building yet, and a bad first chat experience is
  unrecoverable.
- SMS and omnichannel. Email only.
- Auto-shop.
- Calendly plus call recording.
- Binding and payment inside Upline.
- Shopping all appointed markets.
- Writing structured data back into the AMS.
- Live AMS sync and full-book extract at onboarding.
- AMS replacement, and "log in and you're live."
- Accordions on the proposal, Google Docs-style comments, and a 45-second Loom from the agent.
- **Odd policy types.** Motorcycle, RV and toys are out of scope for MVP; farm is harder and also

- Signature and payment links in the final email.
- Referral automation — routing, appointment-setting, pushing to a sales team. "None of this is
  going to be the next 30 days."

**Decided — owner reporting can slip, and we should expect it to look empty.** A customer starting
in September has essentially no data until December.

## 6. The agent experience

The Thursday breadboard is the reference; see [`thu-breadboard.md`](thu-breadboard.md) for the board
as drawn.

**Decided — the weekly operating cadence.** This is the clock the whole product runs on:

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| Weekly pull | Renewals **30–45 days out**, roughly **30 households a week** |
| **Monday 8:00 AM** | The week's list and drafts are ready |
| Mon 8:00 → Tue 9:00 | **48-hour window** for the agent to edit or veto |
| **Tuesday 9:00 AM** | Outreach sends — their data says it is the best send time |

The timeout is what prevents a backlog. You only ever have thirty. Note that Monday readiness is
not the same as making Monday the agency's ritual day, which the room rejected.

**Decided — outreach sends automatically unless the agent intervenes.** Drafts are ready, they go on
schedule, silence means send. We never stack ninety emails in an inbox waiting for approval. The
sales posture is that you are buying a moving train. If a prospect needs to approve every email
forever, they may not be our customer.

**Decided — auto-send also solves list hygiene.** Because the queue moves whether or not the agent
touches it, we do not accumulate a 300-person backlog half of which has expired.

**Decided — deselect is for this renewal only, not a permanent opt-out.** If an agent pulls Bob Jones
out because they want to call him personally, that applies to this renewal. A permanent opt-out would
quietly hollow out the book over time.

**Decided — emails come from the agent's own mailbox, sent individually.** Not a blast from an
`outreach@upline` address. Microsoft first, Google second. A phishing or bulk-mail look is the fear.

**Decided — the screen after the queue is Outreach Review, not a client profile.** It is the email
review, and it carries the client context you need to make a good decision: household, current
policy, renewal amount, phone number, and enough history to know that a 16-year-old driver is what
is driving the increase, or that Stephanie noted a life conversation last week. We are not rebuilding
a CRM.

**Decided — shop results lead with what is different.** Collapse everything identical across
carriers. If it all matches and it is cheaper, it is a dash — "check, check, check, save." Detail is
available on click-through, not on the page by default.

**Decided — talking points and our rationale are the same artifact.** Three to five talking points
come first; the comparison table goes behind a link or dropdown. They should be conversational, not
technical, and explain why we emphasized what we did — "here's how you explain why we raised your
deductible from $500 to $1,000," not "your deductible went up." An LLM does this well.

**Decided — the agent must review the recommendation, and it never auto-sends.** Outreach can be
automatic. The recommendation cannot. AI does not advise the insured.

**Decided — the recommendation editor is WYSIWYG.** The agent sees what the policyholder will see,
edits the copy, and drops carriers they do not want shown. What they approve is what goes out.

**Decided — how many options the policyholder sees is built-in logic, not an agent toggle.** Written
on the board in red. If we shop an acceptable carrier and it saves money, we present that one and
stop. If the incumbent renewal is where they should stay, or rates went up across the board, we show
three and suggest a call.

**Decided — the household snapshot from the questionnaire rides along.** Susie turned sixteen, new
baby, congratulate them.

**Decided — binding stays outside Upline for November 6.** The policyholder approves, the card moves
to the column where the agent has to act, the agent logs into the carrier portal and pulls the
trigger, and marks it closed-won. Approve means *pending*, never bound.

**Decided — the renewal queue is a Kanban.** Not because it must be, but because it is the clearest
way to make a human action impossible to lose.

## 7. The policyholder experience

**Decided — call them the insured or the policyholder, not the customer.** The agency's customer is
the consumer.

**Decided — completing the questionnaire is the trigger.** Not replying, not booking a call, not a
separate "yes, shop me" click in the email.

**Decided — the questionnaire stays a questionnaire for November 6.** We talked about reframing it
into something less form-like and decided not to. In the pilot, 100% of people who clicked the link
and saw the first question finished it. "If they interrupt, don't fix it."

**Decided — the cross-sell question stays on, and is toggleable.** We have no life workflow on day
one, and we keep the question anyway, because it generates leads we can later come back and say
"here are all the leads you took no action on — we have a service for that." Go-to-market owes the
positioning for asking about something we do not yet fulfill.

**Decided — the referral ask lives inside the questionnaire, before shopping.** They have already
felt the value of the ask at that point. On the board it sits alongside verification and cross-sell.

**Decided — the insured gets a landing page, not a jargon PDF and not a comparison crammed into the
email body.** Custom HTML, specific to that household. The email is the invitation; the page is
the brief.

**Decided — the insured-facing surface is agency-branded.** Their logo, no Upline anywhere.

**Decided — the approve button says plainly that it does not put coverage in place.**

**Decided — we say we have access to the markets and used judgment. We do not say we shopped thirty
carriers if we shopped three.** Where it helps, show why we ruled someone out — "they won't write a
pit bull" — rather than manufacturing receipts. The insured wants an expert, not a pile of options.

**Decided — phone stays in the process.** Most agent-to-insured conversation is still phone, not
Zoom. For MVP, booking time is "reply to this email," warm, from the agent's inbox.

## 8. Shopping operations and carriers

**Decided — three carriers per policyholder, shopped by an Upline VA in the carrier portals.**

**Direction set — the agency dictates the carriers by giving us their rules, and we follow them.**
Not per-client picking, which would put another human in the loop and add days. During onboarding we
interview them — "these are your seven carriers, how do you decide?" — and capture the answers as a
shopping-rules markdown file that gates every shop. When they ask why we did not shop someone, we
show them the rule, then update it. The bet is roughly 90% accuracy from that file, and if they want
a fourth carrier shopped, we do another round.

**Why it matters commercially:** training them from day one that we run the shopping is what lets us
later say "we have three carrier partners, this is who you should be shopping." The contract should
say we can shop where we choose, so a future carrier deal does not require renegotiating with every
agency.

**Significant pivot — we are not doing pure apples-to-apples parity shopping.** This follows directly
from percentage-of-premium pricing: if we earn on their premium, our job is to help the book grow.
The shape we landed on is **three options, one of which is parity and one of which is an upsell** —
better coverage at a modestly lower price rather than the cheapest possible number. The agent can
always delete the parity option.

The argument that carried: we just did real work at the insured's request and came back as their
trusted advisor. If there is ever a moment to ask for more, it is now. The alternative is that they
hear nothing and their rates go up anyway.

**Direction set — the VA does a parity pass, then an upsell pass.** "Now you're going to try to get
a 10% increase, starting with these three line items." A quoting guide for VAs is implied and not
yet written.

**Decided — VAs upload the three carrier PDFs; Upline parses them.** This is also our QA hook — it
is how we catch a missed wood stove or a deductible quoted at double.

**Decided — the VA kit, a shared shopping method, and ticketing are required, not nice-to-have.**
They are what keeps the 24-hour promise from being a lie.

**Noted — the progression is VA, then offshore VA, then VA with tooling, then AI shopping.** We are
at the start of it, and the pricing model is deliberately built to reward getting to the end.

## 9. Data, AMS and credentialing

**Decided — the agency gives Upline its own individual AMS login.** Not "use their login." They also
add Upline at every carrier they want shopped. We store credentials, and we are in the AMS at least
weekly.

**Decided — do not name the login `upline@…`.** Use something that looks like the agency.

**Decided — ask for one AMS seat.** AMS is priced per seat.

**Decided — do not integrate with the AMS.** This is strategy, not laziness. No integration
conversation, no "you're replacing EZLynx" on day one, and no API they can switch off.

**Decided — do not write structured data back into the AMS.** An optional compliance dump as a note
is a sales talking point, not a product we want to operate. Two systems is the point; ours has to be
the one they gravitate to.

**Decided — do not deep-map the whole book at onboarding.** It goes stale and it would take weeks.
Census or placeholders only. We write the real household file when we pull that week's renewals.

**Decided — access is the SLA, and it has a clock.** "If you have AMS 360, these ten things in the
first week." Roughly **two weeks to complete access, after which we start charging**. We cannot
start without it. Keep a running per-AMS requirements log so this does not get renegotiated every
time.

**Decided — an onboarding survey, not a series of one-off questions later.** Carrier preferences,
how they prioritize, default send versus hold, ranking philosophy, shopping rules.

**Known hard problem — credentialing.** Shared-login agencies exist. 2FA is a mess: SMS, 1Password
tokens, one carrier requiring Teams plus ID plus Norton. The credential vault has to survive a VA
swap and later an AI.

**Noted, not a build — IBAN is the long game.** One AMS per agency; the day they point IBAN at us we
are the system of record. The hard part is getting them to say they no longer need the old system,
not the integration. Expect the month-13 "turn it off" conversation to leak early, and be ready for
it.

## 10. How we measure success

**Decided — retention is the anchor.** Rolling twelve months, with direction. Everything else is
leading; retention is the lagging outcome we are actually paid for.

**Decided — the two headline numbers are how many households were up for renewal and how many
renewed.** Everything else is supporting. The middle of the funnel does not belong at the top of
the screen.

**Decided — we calculate their trailing-twelve baseline ourselves; we do not take it self-reported.**
Otherwise we cannot honestly claim improvement, and the uplift guarantee has nothing to measure
against.

**Decided — approve does not equal retained.** We verify by pulling from the AMS. The click is too
weak a signal to report on.

**Decided — permissions split by role.** The owner sees the rollup across producers. A producer sees
their own activity only.

**Decided — call out the reporting lag on the surface.** Roughly thirty days. Better to say it than
to have someone conclude the numbers are broken.

**Decided — the month-in-review email is Upline-branded, from Upline to the agent.** This is the one
place we put our own name forward, and it is deliberate: it is the opposite of outreach, which is
always the agent's. It also keeps us a live conversation through year one, ahead of the AMS
conversation.

**Decided — data hygiene counts as a result worth reporting.** "We corrected twelve phone numbers
last month" is both a real win and a seed for the AMS conversation later.

**Decided — kill the funnel presentation.** A descending funnel reads as "only 16 renewed" when the
true story is 116 of 120 renewed and most of them never needed the middle steps. It actively
misleads.

**Decided — the owner view is two halves: what we need you to do, and what we have been doing.**
Above the line is action — bind this policy, review this proposal, jump on this referral — and
clicking an item takes you to where you do it. Below the line is evidence that we have been
protecting the book. Framed as the two questions: "What did you do? What do you need me to do?"

**Decided — the leading metrics we report:** proactive customer touchpoints (of people up for
renewal, what share have we actually reached), percentage who elected to be shopped, number of
customers shopped, cross-sell opportunities surfaced, referrals generated.

**Decided — measure interactions, not tasks completed.** The owner values that we are talking to
their people, independent of whether it converted.

**Decided — low coverage is a feature, not an embarrassment.** "We didn't have an email address, we
should fix that, because they haven't heard from us" is a useful thing to show.

**Decided — we do not report how many people switched carriers.** "No, I care if they stay."

**Decided — separate Upline retention from book retention.** The book number is where the wash and
the bad data live.

**Decided — raw counts over percentages early on.**

**Decided — progressive unlock.** Show what is not populated yet, keep the cadence regular, let
insight open up as data accrues.

**Decided — it is their victory, not ours.** The screen is not a report of our funnel. It is the
communication that we have been protecting their book, and it should make them look good.

## 11. Design principles

**Decided — three magic moments to design first**, from Tuesday's dot vote, in priority order:

1. **Shop results** — "it's doing what we said we were going to do"
2. **The performance dashboard**
3. **The policyholder proposal page** — the biggest design opportunity, and the surface a human will
   see least often, so it has to land the first time

Referrals came in as a stretch fourth ("3A") for Amanda and Ashley if there was time.

**Decided — one experience, not twenty pages.** The complaint about the incumbents is breadcrumbs
five levels deep. Clicking a client from the queue slides in or drills into the same surface and
returns you to the queue with the status changed.

**Decided — familiar plus unexpected.** It should match enough of what an insurance person expects
that they are not asking "what is this," while still feeling different from everyone else claiming
to do this.

**Decided — we build in delight deliberately.** The thing everyone remembers about Pipedrive is the
golfing animation. Celebration on closed-won, sound effects, animation. "We should definitely plan
to have some fun with it."

**Decided — do not add humans to the flow until we have to.** Every checkpoint, toggle, and setting
we add is a place the product stalls. The anti-goal is a system that only works because the customer
poured their time into configuring it.

**The governing question from the breadboard**, worth keeping on the wall:

> What would make an agent stop? What do they want to have their fingerprints on?

## 13. Still open

Nothing here is decided. Please do not build against any of it.

**Pricing**

- The actual percentage. 0.5% probably does not cover shopping; likely 0.6–0.7%. **Owed: a fully
  loaded VA cost model.**
- The uplift guarantee threshold — what retention increase we are promising.
- Whether we present both options or gate Option B.
- At what book size we stop publishing a price and go custom.
- Whether there is a referral or revenue-share component at all.

**Product**

- Whether the recommendation is an email, a visual, or both. The board still reads "Email / Visual?"
- Which send variant is the default: (A) rate change at the same price, or (B) same coverage at a
  better price.
- Where a completed recommendation logs back to. The board asks "Biz Dash or Renewal Queue?" —
  cutting the dashboard answers it as the queue by default, but we never said so.
- Whether the action center *is* the home screen with the dashboard beneath it, or a separate view
  of the same queue. Jacob pushed for one screen; handed to Amanda unresolved.
- Whether the monthly month-with-Upline email survives alongside the screen. Amanda wants it, to

## 14. Risks we named out loud

**Sold does not equal delivered.** Named the number-one risk at the open on Tuesday. We sell a
journey, they use it, and it is not the journey they bought.

**We add work instead of removing it.** Called "the biggest risk of the whole venture." If the agent
ends up making more decisions and spending more time just to retain customers who would have stayed
anyway, we have made their life worse. The only answer is proving we meaningfully move retention.

**False purchase.** The insured clicks approve, or thinks they did, the agent never binds, and then
there is a claim. This is why approve means pending, why the language on the button is explicit, and
why "needs binding" has to age visibly and cannot clear itself.

**No AMS access, no product.** Credentialing is the key to the kingdom and it is operationally nasty.

**The 24-hour shop promise is already in the deck** and the VA system to deliver it does not exist
yet.

**Two systems.** We are asking owners to tolerate the AMS plus Upline while we make ours the one they
actually open. Some will refuse a second source of truth.

**The value chain breaks at the proposal.** If we shop and send a recommendation the agent would have
built differently, and they cannot change it, they disengage. The WYSIWYG editor exists because of
this.

**Upselling could cost them a customer.** We are choosing to push coverage up rather than shop pure
parity. Done badly, that loses the insured. This one should be experimented on, not assumed —
Austin's suggestion was splitting the population into thirds and testing.
