**The decision this comes from:** Thursday afternoon, Sept 10. Jacob named four lanes — **people,
capital, customers, product** — and asked for 90-day OKRs in each. Ashley's to-do out of that room:
*"I do a first bad pass at the OKRs and our first team meeting is we all go through and then revise
that. Because it kind of summarizes this week at the same time."*

Source: [Upline Thursday Afternoon Transcript - Timeline, Lanes and Cadence](strategy-sprint/Upline%20Thursday%20Afternoon%20Transcript%20-%20Timeline,%20Lanes%20and%20Cadence.md),
sections 19 and 20.

**This replaces the one-pager.** The synthesis of sprint week is not a summary document — it is this
set of OKRs. The decisions digest ([`sprint-week/decisions.md`](sprint-week/decisions.md)) is the
record of what we agreed; the OKRs are what we are committing to do about it.

## The frame

**Four lanes.** Jacob's naming, with the translation to how we have been talking about them:

| Lane | What it covers | Where it already lives |
|---|---|---|
| **People** | Hiring, VA operations, who owns what, operating cadence | [`action-tracker.md`](../action-tracker.md) §H, Thursday transcript §12–13, §15 |
| **Capital** | Investor readiness, SPV, data room, contracting, company insurance | Thursday transcript §5–8, §10 |
| **Customers** | Demos, contracts, pilots, paid social, conferences | [`timelines.md`](timelines.md) Part 2, [`../go-to-market/next-steps.md`](../go-to-market/next-steps.md) |
| **Product** | The Nov 6 MVP and everything gating it | [`timelines.md`](timelines.md) Part 1, [`sprint-week/decisions.md`](sprint-week/decisions.md) §5–11 |

"Customers" is the go-to-market lane. "Capital" is the investor and legal lane. Use Jacob's words in
the room so we are all naming the same four things.

**The period: Sept 15 – Dec 31.** Fifteen weeks, not literally ninety days. Every fixed date the team
has already committed to lands inside it — Nov 6 launch, 120 demos by Nov 30, ~30 contracts by Dec 31,
investor commitments by Oct 15. Anything shorter cuts the contract number in half; anything longer
invents commitments we have not made.

**The bar for a key result**, in Jacob's words: *"Your key results are very specific. You'll know when
we got done or not."* Two tests before anything goes on the page:

1. **Is it a result or an activity?** "Stand up Linear" is an activity. "All seven above-the-line
   surfaces shipped and running a real renewal week" is a result. Most of what is in the action
   tracker today is activity — that is correct for a tracker and wrong for a key result.
2. **Is the number knowable on Dec 31 without a debate?** If two people could look at the same data
   and disagree about whether we hit it, it is not written tightly enough.

**Less is more.** This is the part Jacob was most emphatic about: *"That's where people fail. 'We can
do this. We can do this. We can do this.' No, you can't. You can only do a handful."* The target is
**one objective and three key results per lane — twelve key results total, hard cap sixteen.** The
discipline is in what gets left off.

**What the OKRs are not.** They are not a re-statement of the timeline. Nov 6 and 120 demos are
already the spine of the plan and nobody is going to forget them. The useful work is picking up the
things that are currently unowned or unmeasured and giving them a number.

## Step 2 — Draft, deliberately rough

**Done, Sept 17.** Ashley’s pre-Thursday draft is cataloged in [`2026-q4-okrs.md`](2026-q4-okrs.md)
and on Through Line `#/okrs`. That is the artifact the room edits. This file stays the process.

One objective per lane, written as a sentence a person would say out loud. Three key results under it,
each with a number, a date, and a named owner.

Write it fast and do not polish. The instruction in the room was a first bad pass that the team tears
up — a draft that looks finished invites approval instead of argument, which is the opposite of what
the session is for.

**Cut rule when a lane has more than three candidates:** keep the one that is a lagging outcome
(retention, contracts, money committed) and the two that are most likely to be missed if nobody is
watching. Drop anything already load-bearing in the timeline, because it does not need a second home.

## Step 3 — Presentation format

**Draft in the repo, present from the repo, publish to Through Line after it is ratified.**

- **Draft:** `project-planning/2026-q4-okrs.md` — markdown, one page, four sections
- **Tuesday's session:** present from that file directly so edits can be made live in the room.
  A deck cannot be revised while eight people argue about a number; a markdown file can.
- **After ratification:** publish as a page on Through Line. That is the shareable artifact that
  replaces the one-pager, and it is the format the monthly report-out reuses.

---

## Step 4 — The working session

**First weekly, Thursday Sept 17. OKR revision is the whole agenda — do not also try to run status.**

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Why we are doing this, the bar for a key result, and the less-is-more rule. Say out loud that the draft is expected to be wrong. |
| 10 min | **People** — read the objective, argue the three, confirm the owner |
| 10 min | **Capital** |
| 10 min | **Customers** |
| 10 min | **Product** |
| 10 min | What got cut and what we are deliberately not measuring. Confirm the monthly review date. |

**Rules for the room:** every key result leaves with a name attached. If a lane cannot get to three
that meet the bar, it leaves with two — an empty slot is more honest than a soft one. Anything that
turns into a twenty-minute debate gets a follow-up, not a compromise number.

---

## Step 5 — What happens to them afterward

- The OKRs become **the format for the monthly report-out** and fold into the board cadence.
- The [action tracker](../action-tracker.md) stays the week-to-week detail. The OKRs sit above it —
  if something in the tracker does not ladder to a key result, that is worth noticing.
- Reviewed monthly, not weekly. The weekly reviews movement; the month reviews the commitment.
