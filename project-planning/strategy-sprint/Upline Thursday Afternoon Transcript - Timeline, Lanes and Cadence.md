## What came out of the room

**The frame:** four lanes — **people, capital, customers, product**. Everything else in the session hangs off those, and the first weekly meeting will be spent turning them into 90-day OKRs. "Less is more. That's where people fail."

**Product timeline — the backwards plan from Nov 6:**
- Six weeks of dev starting immediately, three weeks of design overlapping the front of it, one to two weeks of QA before launch. Two weeks of QA is the ideal; one is the fallback if things get tight.
- Stockton Hill: set up Tuesday/Wednesday of next week, kickoff **Friday Sept 18**, running three weeks.
- Design partner is **Landry** — already identified, Davey talking to him that day. Starts around **Oct 2** and dovetails into launch. Ashley expects to shift the start later so the three weeks land against the QA window rather than ahead of it.
- Ashley expects Stockton Hill setup to cost her about a day, after which most of her time goes to design and user experience with Amanda.
- Feature-level detail goes into Linear, not into this room.

**Go-to-market:**
- Paid social v1 out **late next week**, then a standing check-in cadence on the campaign and additional content. Warm-up posting already started the night before.
- A new website version at the end of next week — possibly just a new homepage. Amanda flagged she needs half a week of dedicated time, and product may need to slide roughly half a week to get it.
- Pitch deck iteration and a press release drafted Friday, handed off for a next pass, design polish after.
- New pricing hits the pitch deck hardest, is lighter on the website (no pricing listed), and could be the whole message of some paid-social videos rather than a line in all of them.

**Investor lane — the hard date is September 27:**
- Everything investor-facing ready before the conference: pitch deck, incorporation docs, term sheet, and a data room, so a hallway conversation can end with "can you share that with me."
- Content edits on the deck done by **Sept 21**, leaving a week for design.
- **SPV** framing done by Sept 27 so JB's and Davey's network can come in through one vehicle. The SPV does not have to be fully figured out — the event is about soliciting interest.
- **Commitments by Oct 15.** Revenue is a separate lane; pre-selling starts before then and launch is with revenue in October.
- Still missing from the board: investor guidance, investor CRM, an outreach cadence, and swim lanes for IR.

**Commercial and legal:**
- Design partner is treated as free pilot, rolling into paid Nov 6. Open question whether that needs real paper or the existing studio-style agreement.
- Contracting work stream kicks off next week: a 60-minute call to lay out terms and concerns (12- or 24-month, pricing mechanics, data protections), output into a GPT summary, then a session with counsel.
- **Bobby Weinberger's group** (California) for the SaaS template, **Fenwick** for the insurance-specific language — roughly $1,000 for the coaching pass instead of a $10,000 drafting exercise, three or four grand all in.
- **The company needs its own cyber and data liability now**, not later. Historically written on the studio; with employees and this data profile, it needs its own structure. Bundles with the workers' comp shopping that has to happen anyway.
- Regulatory constraint, stated plainly: a percentage of premium cannot be tied to a specific policy and cannot be named on a commission account. **The word "commission" cannot appear in the documents.** Precedent: credit unions aren't licensed either and get paid a "market access fee."
- Contract execution at demo-to-contract scale will be manual DocuSign first, product-embedded later. Unowned: who preps, QAs, routes, and files signed documents, and what turnaround time we hold ourselves to.
- Billing mechanics are unresolved — a monthly true-up against actual premium may not fit Stripe cleanly.

**People:**
- **Decision on the VA hire:** hire a senior manager–level VA in the country where we intend to build the team, treat them as a core Upline team member (title, LinkedIn, exclusivity), and expect them to build the process and hire the next ten. Not a US W-2 employee. **Davey owns the relationship and is who they report to;** Austin runs process, tooling, and shopping protocols as a consultant, explicitly not as the bottleneck.
- Target: offer out by **Sept 21** or that week. Davey and Austin review applications together next week.
- **Blocker:** employment agreements, waiting on Zach.
- The studio is recruiting a head of product with a couple of people in the pipeline. No urgency given the current team; the Upline team will be pulled into interviews if someone compelling appears.

**Cadence — decided:**
- **Kill the Monday How Should We.** This team runs the weekly from here on.
- One weekly all-hands on **Tuesday** (Monday is bad for pulling campaign analytics; Wednesday is bad for someone else), then split into product and go-to-market tracks. Product runs its own faster cadence, likely dailies; marketing/sales runs a separate deeper session.
- Structure it so people aren't held for the whole hour — headline plus implications, with a deliberate order so the trickle effect between lanes is visible.
- Ashley drafts the weekly's structure and circulates for feedback.
- Board: one board member for now, proper board deferred past the seed until institutional money. Real board cadence anyway, plus a **monthly business review / report-out** in OKR format.
- In-person: a few days together before launch, ideally overlapping the start or tail of QA — "submitting a crazy amount of tickets for QA virtually is just not ideal." Getting it on the books now, plus a minimum quarterly rhythm.
- Ticketing in Linear; stand-ups carry the technical detail. Trying **open spec** so the specification is the source of truth and Ashley isn't hand-updating documents for everyone.

**The homework:** Ashley does a deliberately rough first pass at OKRs across the four lanes; the first weekly meeting is the team tearing it up and revising. That doubles as the synthesis of sprint week. The decisions digest goes out rather than being read aloud.

**Amanda's prototype:** a Claude-built clickthrough of the whole flow — renewal queue (combo view), outreach review, questionnaire, shopping, recommendation email, celebration moment, business intelligence screen. **Session note:** the last twenty minutes are reflections. Recurring themes — the pricing shift as the week's breakthrough, how different the process feels post-Opus 4.6 ("we would have shown these sketches on paper"), and that a team that met this week does not feel like a team that met this week.
